# 0043 — Review-Surface Boundary Contract (DR-W6-06)

**Status:** Accepted by human reviewer, 2026-08-17. Not a build instruction. Authorises no implementation.
**Date:** August 2026 · **Phase:** W6 — Surface Era · **Deliverable:** **W6-D1**
**Position:** the sixth and final W6-D1 doctrine record, carrying the brief's law 15: the review-surface boundary contract — what a future surface may read, what it must never do, and what it must prove before it exists. **This record gates W6-D4 and does not open it. Its acceptance completes the W6-D1 doctrine set; doctrine complete is not capability begun.** It creates no surface, no cache, no store, no workflow, no path; it renders nothing and reads nothing.
**Constitutional references:** W0 Laws 1, 3, 8, 11, 13; W0 Non-Goal 7. **No law is amended.**
**North star carried:** governed truth must survive display without becoming authority.
**Resolves:** none.

---

**Five records taught the words and the light; this one bounds the window. A review surface is a place where a human looks at governed truth — and the whole contract is that looking is all the place can do. It reads what the boundary shows it, writes nothing anywhere, remembers nothing between glances, invents no channel, performs no ceremony, and records no human act. The W5 runtime proved a boundary can be real because it refuses; the surface era ends its ink the same way: the window is a window, structurally — never a door.**

## Decision question

**What is a review surface, what may it read, what must it never write, hold, invent, or perform, what must it declare, and what must any future W6-D4 surface prove before it exists — such that a surface remains a bounded reader over governed sources and can never become a source, a store, a judge, a workflow, or a second system?**

## Controlling law

- **The accepted W6-D1 brief** — law 15 assigned here, with its boundary language carried verbatim: **read-only against governed sources; no write-back, no cache, no annotation store, no state, no second data channel.**
- **ADR-0038** — V4 and V5, the visual declaration obligations of decision 15, class-as-data, and the closed display vocabulary: a surface shows catalogue-lawful labels in declared inventories, and nothing else.
- **ADR-0039, consumed whole** — the formula (**display may preserve source authority; display may not create authority**), rule 12's declarability admission condition, bind (xi)'s no-machine-verdict, and the conformance-proof pattern of its decision 10, which this record completes for surfaces.
- **ADR-0040 decisions 3–12** — the per-state rendering conformance set a surface must satisfy for every honest state it shows.
- **ADR-0041 decisions 3–15** — the routed-material conformance set: whole deltas, unconverted stubs, living questions, the attribution chain.
- **ADR-0042** — catalogue identity and ID law: a surface uses catalogue entries by ID with their metadata, and an ID authorises nothing by itself; the W6-D2 gate stands untouched.
- **The W5 runtime and composition posture, as precedent only** — boundary properties held by structural proof (no-capability AST scans, refusal codes, statelessness, registered operations); the same proof style binds future surfaces, and **no runtime file changes here or ever under this record**.
- **The pending ledger's no-bulk-approve obligation** and **ADR-0024 decisions 13–14** — observation is never a second data channel; the surface is the presentation twin of that law.

## Decisions

### Part A — The door and the consumption

1. **This record decides the surface contract and creates no surface.** Nothing here reads, renders, stores, or connects; every obligation below binds artefacts that do not yet exist.

2. **Consumption, exact:** ADR-0038's V4/V5, declaration obligations, class-as-data and closed vocabulary; ADR-0039 whole; ADR-0040 decisions 3–12; ADR-0041 decisions 3–15; ADR-0042's identity, ID and gate law; the ledger's no-bulk-approve; the brief's boundary language; the W5 proof-style precedent. Nothing consumed is weakened, and conflicts resolve upward through the consumed records in their own order.

### Part B — Identity and non-identity

3. **A review surface is: a bounded reader over governed sources and governed catalogue entries, presented to a human for inspection or review.** It is a **presentation boundary, never an authority boundary**: everything true on the surface was true in the sources, and nothing becomes true by being shown.

4. **A review surface is not:** a source of truth · a source of authority · a runtime · a model · a memory · a cache · an annotation store · a decision store · a workflow engine · an approval system · a conversion system · a retirement system · a human-review record · a hidden ledger · a fifth channel · or a substitute for source records, catalogue records, or human acts. **Sixteen refusals, each structural once a surface exists: the artefact must be provably unable to be any of them, not merely styled to avoid them.**

### Part C — Read law

5. **A surface may read only governed sources exposed to it through a declared boundary** — declared inputs, read through what the sources already expose, in the ADR-0024 sense: the surface recovers nothing the boundary was not built to show.

6. **No discovery.** A surface may not acquire sources by path walk, search, heuristic inference, model inference, metadata sweep, or convenience. An undeclared input is not an integration; it is a breach.

7. **No merging into unstated sources.** A surface may compose a view from declared inputs, but may not synthesise a new source — no derived dataset, no computed summary presented as if a record held it, no aggregation that outruns its inputs' authority (ADR-0039 decision 5 applies to views exactly as to labels).

8. **No model output, in this era.** A surface may not read model output unless a future W7-era governed record explicitly creates such a source — **and no W6 record does.**

### Part D — Write law

9. **A surface writes nothing, anywhere.** Not to source records, catalogue entries, the registry, the pending ledger, evaluation records, fixtures, Lane C, Tier 3, applicability records, human-decision records, or the runtime — and not around them either: no export-that-mutates, no touch-on-read, no reordering persisted back.

10. **A surface persists nothing.** No derived labels, filters, review choices, clicked items, selections, comments, annotations, human decisions, or display state may be persisted unless a future governed record creates a lawful store with its own schema, ceremony, and proofs. **In W6-D1, no such store exists, and this record creates none.**

### Part E — State law

11. **Transient render-time state only, and only if later authorised.** A future surface may hold the ephemeral state strictly necessary to display a view — if its own accepted brief authorises it and its proofs show that state neither persists nor mutates any governed record. Beyond that: **no cache may become a shadow source · no local convenience state may become evidence · no hidden queue may become a review workflow · no annotation field may become a human decision.** Each of those four drifts is named here so no implementation can wander into one innocently.

### Part F — Channel law

12. **There are no hidden channels. There is no fifth channel.** The four observed surfaces of the evaluation era were a closed set at the boundary; the presentation era inherits the same discipline: a surface may not create a side channel for approvals, conversions, exceptions, telemetry, analytics, interaction history, human notes, or model outputs without its own governed crossing record. **If a future surface needs a channel, that is not a styling choice — it is new architecture, and it must stop** and route to its own governed record before a line of it exists.

### Part G — Declarability law

13. **Any future review-surface artefact declares, as data:** its source inputs · its source-to-display derivations · the catalogue entries used, by ID · label classes · mandatory accompaniments · visual semantics · glyph inventory · colour-semantic mapping · default coverage · filters and collapse rules · ordering and grouping rules · affordance inventory · **write paths, if any — which are none unless later governed** · **persistence paths, if any — which are none unless later governed** · **external paths, if any — which are none unless later governed.** Fifteen declarations, one admission rule: **no declaration, no pass** (ADR-0039 rule 12, completed for surfaces).

### Part H — No bulk, no ceremony bypass

14. **The no-bulk-approve obligation is a surface-contract term:** no approve-all · no convert-all · no resolve-all · no retire-all · no mark-done batch · no multi-select that implies ceremony can be collapsed · and no affordance of any kind that performs or implies approval, conversion, retirement, or resolution without its own governed ceremony (ADR-0038 V5 and ADR-0041 decisions 8–9, carried into the contract as structural obligations).

### Part I — The human-review boundary

15. **A surface may show that a human review is needed; it may show a human act only where a governed source records one; it may never create or record one** — recording a human act requires a future governed record creating the store, the schema, and the ceremony, and none exists. **A surface may not make review feel complete because a person has seen the item.** The four aphorisms are contract terms: **viewing is not reviewing · clicking is not deciding · selecting is not approving · closing a panel is not retiring.** No interaction with a display is a governed act, and no display may imply otherwise.

### Part J — Proof obligations for future W6-D4 surfaces

16. **The proof set any future review surface must satisfy before it exists — specifications only; the proofs execute later, when a surface artefact exists, in the W5 same-commit style:** source-input declaration proof · no-undeclared-source proof · read-only source proof · no-write-back proof · no-cache/store proof · no-annotation-store proof · no-hidden-state proof · no-fifth-channel proof · label inventory proof (closed vocabulary, classes as data) · catalogue-ID usage proof (entries by ID, IDs authorising nothing) · mandatory accompaniment proof · visual inventory proof · V1–V6 prohibited-visual proof · coverage/no-concealment proof · ordering/non-quality proof · affordance/no-ceremony-bypass proof · no-machine-verdict proof · no-human-act-invention proof · no-bulk-approve proof · and no model/network/vendor/hosted path proof unless separately governed. **Twenty obligations; a surface that cannot carry them is not incomplete — it is unlawful.**

### Part K — W6-D1 completion posture

17. **This is the sixth and final W6-D1 doctrine record.** At its acceptance and publication, the acceptance criteria of the W6-D1 brief's §4 are satisfied: all six decision records (ADR-0038 through ADR-0043) accepted and published through full ceremony, each before what it gates; all fourteen runway doctrine questions answered in exactly one record each; all ten risk bindings discharged into records; the visual-semantics doctrine binding in DR-W6-01 and DR-W6-04; the deterministic proof obligations stated in the records that own them; and no catalogue, grading record, rendered label, or surface existing anywhere. **W6-D1 is therefore doctrine-complete at this record's publication — and doctrine complete is not capability begun.** W6-D2 becomes eligible for its own future accepted brief and **is not opened by this record**; W6-D3 through W6-D6 likewise remain unopened behind their own gates.

### Part L — Boundaries

18. **This record does not decide:** catalogue artefact shape or entries · final catalogue IDs · string grading or D5-T24 execution · rendered labels · UI design · review-surface implementation · any human review workflow, decision store, or approval/conversion/retirement ceremony · runtime changes · model contact · W6-D2, W6-D3, or W6-D4 implementation.

19. **W6-D2 through W6-D6 remain unopened; W7 remains unopened.** No stub converts, no question retires, no review occurs, no human act is invented, no cache, store, workflow, or path of any kind is created, no fixture, evaluation record, Lane C row, Tier 3 row, or applicability record changes, no model is contacted, and no dependency is added.

## Alternatives considered

- **A stateful surface with a "harmless" preferences store (rejected).** Preferences become defaults, defaults become concealment, and a store becomes a shadow source; state arrives only through a future governed record or not at all.
- **Letting surfaces cache for performance by default (rejected).** A cache is a copy with opinions about freshness; the freshness ladder lives in the engine, and a stale cache rendering as current would be display creating authority.
- **An interaction log "just for usability" (rejected firmly).** Interaction history is a behavioural dataset about a person by another name; W1-D1's ledger rule reaches presentation whole.
- **Allowing soft batch actions with confirmation dialogs (rejected, ruled).** A confirmed bulk approval is still a bulk approval; ceremony does not collapse into a modal.
- **Deferring the channel question to implementation (rejected).** The fifth channel is always discovered under deadline; it is barred now, by name, while no deadline exists.

## Consequences

- The W6-D1 doctrine set closes complete: vocabulary, firewall, state law, routing law, catalogue law, and surface contract — six records, fourteen questions, ten risks, all in force before anything displays.
- W6-D4 inherits a twenty-proof specification and a declarability gate; W6-D2 inherits its eligibility; and every future surface argument is now a checklist, not a negotiation.
- The Wing's presentation layer is bounded before it exists — the same order of operations that made the runtime trustworthy.

## Constitutional check

- **Law 1** — a surface contacts no one, runs nothing in the background, and displays invitations, never pushes.
- **Law 3** — nothing shown becomes true by being shown; the surface can neither mint nor launder authority.
- **Law 8** — no inference path opens: no discovery, no merging, no model reading, no fifth channel.
- **Law 11** — no person is gated: viewing states never conditions anyone's rights, and no interaction is an act.
- **Law 13** — declarations are data; every surface obligation is auditable at the artefact that bears it.
- **W0 Non-Goal 7** — no surface can be dressed as, or perform, a certification-style claim.
- **No new authority, no new namespace, no new store, no new channel** — sixteen non-identities, held structurally.
