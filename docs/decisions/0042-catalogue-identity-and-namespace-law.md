# 0042 — Catalogue Identity and Catalogue-ID Namespace Law (DR-W6-05)

**Status:** Accepted by human reviewer, 2026-08-17. Not a build instruction. Authorises no implementation.
**Date:** August 2026 · **Phase:** W6 — Surface Era · **Deliverable:** **W6-D1**
**Position:** the fifth W6-D1 doctrine record, carrying the brief's law 14: what the governed string catalogue **is**, what a catalogue ID **means**, how the namespace behaves, and what W6-D2 must prove before any catalogue artefact can exist. **This record gates W6-D2 and does not open it.** It creates no catalogue, no file, no schema, no entry; it allocates no final catalogue ID; it grades no string; it wakes no validator; it renders nothing.
**Constitutional references:** W0 Laws 3, 11, 13; W0 Non-Goal 7. **No law is amended.**
**North star carried:** governed truth must survive display without becoming authority.
**Resolves:** none.

---

**Before the Wing's approved words can live anywhere, this record decides what the place is. The catalogue is a register, not a voice: it holds strings the way the registry holds records — governed, cited, auditable — and it lends nothing to what it holds. A catalogue ID is a name, not a nod: it identifies one string record and means nothing else, ranks nothing, approves nothing, and authorises nothing by existing. The thirty-three strings that waited two phases to be governed will wait one deliverable more — because the register they enter must be law before it is a file.**

## Decision question

**What is the governed string catalogue, what does a catalogue ID identify and never imply, how does the namespace stay closed, stable, and meaning-free beyond identity, what must the woken validator check, and what must W6-D2 prove before the first catalogue artefact may exist?**

## Controlling law

- **The accepted W6-D1 brief** — law 14 assigned here; the statement, carried in force, that **no catalogue artefact and no final catalogue ID exists in W6-D1**; the DR-W4-06 decidability discipline for validator obligations.
- **ADR-0038** — decisions 4, 10 and 12 consumed as catalogue validation obligations (class-as-data; the barred display list with the `certif-` and `complian-` stem bans, consumable verbatim; mechanical checkability landing with the first string-bearing artefact); the seven vocabulary classes as the only lawful memberships.
- **ADR-0039, consumed whole** — the formula (**display may preserve source authority; display may not create authority**) applied to registration itself: cataloguing a string lends it nothing; and **rule 12's declarability admission condition**, which the catalogue must satisfy about itself before anything it holds can.
- **ADR-0040 and ADR-0041** — the accompaniment tables and per-state obligations the catalogue will carry as content: accompaniments are catalogue members with the same governance as the labels they accompany.
- **The W4/W5 closure carry-forwards** — the thirty-three room-register strings (eleven Wellness, eleven Kitchen, eleven Gym, zero Meditation — the zero a source-derived finding) held as review-of-record; the **dormant catalogue-ID validator class** (`check_m13_catalogue_dormancy` today asserts dormancy only), dependency-named and never reported as passing.
- **The register precedent** — W4-D6-EASR's allocation-identifier semantics: stable allocation order, immutable once accepted, never a ranking — the namespace shape this record adopts.

## Decisions

### Part A — Catalogue identity and non-identity

1. **This record decides catalogue law and creates no catalogue.** Nothing here is a file, a schema, an entry, an ID, or a wake of anything dormant.

2. **The catalogue is:** a **governed register of approved display strings and their required metadata** for later presentation artefacts — each entry a governed string record carrying, as data: its string, its ADR-0038 vocabulary class, its source authority, its derivation rule reference where derived, its accompaniment linkage, its prohibition register where applicable, and its proof obligations. The catalogue is to display what the registry is to records: the place where governance can see every word the Wing is permitted to show.

3. **The catalogue is not:** a surface · a UI · a renderer · a copy deck · a product glossary · a design system · a translation layer that could soften source states (softening is ADR-0038's authority-by-thesaurus, barred at the register itself) · **and never a source of authority** — registration lends a string nothing, and a catalogued string displays lawfully only where every other law already permits it.

### Part B — Membership and exclusion

4. **The catalogue may later contain governed strings only**, in the seven ADR-0038 classes: display labels · mandatory accompaniments (the ADR-0040/0041 tables as first-class members) · non-authority notices · routing labels · review labels · prohibition labels · future-owned labels · and source-bounded explanatory strings **only if W6-D2's own accepted brief lawfully admits them** under ADR-0038 decision 9.

5. **The catalogue must never contain:** source records themselves · evidence records · fixture records · evaluation results · human decisions (except, and only under later law, as source-cited display strings per ADR-0041 decision 6) · UI layout · glyphs · colour mappings · affordance behaviour · screenshots · runtime behaviour · model output · **or anything that would create a surface by being registered.** The catalogue holds words about states; it never holds the states, the pixels, or the behaviours.

### Part C — Catalogue-ID law

6. **A catalogue ID identifies one governed string record — and that is the whole of its meaning.** It is: **stable** once accepted · **immutable** except through an explicit supersession or retirement record (supersession visible, identifiers never reused — the W4-D6-BEF rule carried) · and **meaning-free beyond identity**: it never encodes ranking, state quality, room priority, risk level, confidence, approval, or display order.

7. **A catalogue ID must never imply:** acceptance · safety · correctness · satisfaction · any `certif-` or `complian-` form · legal satisfaction · diagnosis · therapy · production readiness · or proof. **An ID never functions as a verdict, never substitutes for source citation, and never authorises display by itself** — presence in the catalogue is a necessary condition for a string's later display, never a sufficient one.

### Part D — Namespace law

8. **The namespace is closed and mechanically checkable.** Its grammar is fixed here without allocating anything: **a single flat sequence — the `CAT-` prefix followed by a zero-padded allocation number** (illustratively `CAT-0001`, **non-final, illustrative only — no catalogue ID is allocated by this record**). Allocation order is the register precedent's: stable, next-unused, immutable, never a ranking, never chronology-as-priority.

9. **Identity and meaning are structurally separated:** the ID identifies the catalogue record; **every meaning lives in the record's fields** — class, source, derivation, accompaniment, prohibition, proof obligations. Nothing semantic is encoded in the ID by design, so no ID can ever leak class, room, state, or quality — and the grammar admits no variant that could. Reserved: the `CAT-` prefix itself, for governed string records alone; no sub-namespaces, class prefixes, or room prefixes exist, and creating one would be a future governed record's act, not a formatting choice.

### Part E — The seed corpus posture

10. **The thirty-three review-of-record strings are the seed corpus for W6-D2 — and nothing here touches them.** Their existence is inventory, not admission: they are **not graded, not accepted into any catalogue, and not renderable** by anything in W6-D1. **W6-D2 must ingest or reject each one through its own accepted brief, its validation, and its ceremony** — and rejection is a lawful outcome; a seed is a candidate, never a passenger. The Meditation zero remains a source-derived finding and is displayed, when anything displays, as exactly that.

### Part F — Validator obligations

11. **When W6-D2 wakes the dormant catalogue-ID validator class — by record, through its own ceremony, never before — it must check, deterministically:** ID grammar conformance · uniqueness with no reuse · **no final ID existing anywhere before W6-D2's authority** · class present as data on every record · class within the seven lawful ADR-0038 classes · barred-word and stem absence in every display-string field, with the prohibition register the only exception (ADR-0038 decisions 10–12 consumed verbatim) · mandatory accompaniment linkage present wherever ADR-0040/0041 require one · derivation rule reference present wherever the class is derived · source-bounded citation present wherever ADR-0038 decision 9 applies · supersession and retirement semantics honoured where any exist · no ranking, order, or priority encoded in any ID · **no catalogue record without accepted source authority** · and **no display-authorisation claim arising from ID presence alone.** Until that wake, the class stays exactly as it is today — a dormancy assertion, dependency-named, never reported as passing — and this record does not touch it.

### Part G — The W6-D2 gate

12. **W6-D2 may open only after this record is accepted and published — and this record does not open it.** W6-D2 still requires its own accepted brief through its own ceremony, and must prove at its own landings, before any catalogue artefact exists: the artefact satisfies ADR-0039 rule 12's declarability condition about itself · every entry satisfies Part F's checks with the woken validator green in the same commit · every ingested seed string passed its own admission decision on the record · no entry claims or receives authority by registration · and the catalogue file's own home, format, and registry posture are stated in its brief rather than assumed. **No catalogue file, schema, data file, fixture, or validator activation appears in W6-D1** — and none appears in this landing.

### Part H — Boundaries

13. **This record does not decide:** the final catalogue artefact shape, home, or format · any actual catalogue entry · any final catalogue ID · review-of-record string grading or D5-T24 execution (DR-W6-03's sibling deliverable W6-D3, and its own records) · label rendering · UI · review surfaces · the review-surface boundary contract (DR-W6-06) · W6-D2, W6-D3, or W6-D4 implementation.

14. **W6-D1 remains open; W6-D2 through W6-D6 remain unopened; W7 remains unopened.** No stub converts, no question retires, no string is graded or ingested, no validator wakes, no fixture, evaluation record, Lane C row, Tier 3 row, or applicability record changes, no model is contacted, and no dependency is added.

## Alternatives considered

- **Class- or room-prefixed catalogue IDs (rejected).** Encoding meaning in identity is how an ID becomes a claim; the flat sequence keeps every meaning in governed fields where the validator can check it.
- **Auto-ingesting the thirty-three strings as founding entries (rejected firmly).** Admission without validation would make the seed corpus the only ungoverned words in the register; each string earns entry through W6-D2's own ceremony or is rejected on the record.
- **Letting the catalogue carry rendering hints (rejected).** Glyphs, colours, and affordances are surface declarations under ADR-0038 decision 15; a register that carries pixels has become a design system and lost its identity.
- **Treating ID presence as display authorisation (rejected, ruled).** Necessary, never sufficient — otherwise the register itself would mint the authority the firewall exists to prevent.
- **Waking the validator class now, in doctrine (rejected).** Capability follows governance: the wake is W6-D2's landing, with its proofs, in its commit.

## Consequences

- W6-D2 inherits a complete gate: what the catalogue is, what it may hold, what its IDs mean, what its validator must prove, and what its brief must decide — nothing left to invent under deadline.
- The thirty-three strings finally have a stated path: seed corpus → per-string admission or rejection → governed entries under a woken validator.
- The namespace question is closed before the first ID exists, so no allocation can ever carry a hidden meaning.

## Constitutional check

- **Law 3** — registration lends nothing: an ID is a name, not a nod, and the record says so in terms.
- **Law 11** — no person is gated: catalogue records describe display strings, never people.
- **Law 13** — every entry, class, source, and linkage is data; the register is as auditable as the registry it mirrors.
- **W0 Non-Goal 7** — no ID and no entry can constitute or imply a certification-style claim.
- **No new authority** — one namespace is defined and deliberately left empty; nothing is allocated, woken, or implemented.

## Non-goals

This record authorises no catalogue, no catalogue file, no schema, no entry, no final catalogue ID, no string grading, no D5-T24 execution, no rendered label, no UI, no CLI, no review surface, no validator wake or edit, no runtime, fixture, evaluation-record, pending-ledger, Lane C, Tier 3 or applicability change, no human review, no human decision record, no model contact, no generative evaluation, no dependency, no E10/vendor, Z4, E12/Z5 or hosted movement, no W6-D2 through W6-D6 opening, and no W7 opening. ## Public-safety considerations

Generic and structural wording — catalogue, register, string, record, class, source, validator. Barred vocabulary appears only inside prohibitions, with the two scan-sensitive families carried as stems. The single illustrative identifier is marked non-final in place. No real user data, no vendor or model names, no URLs, and no claim of any kind about any person, string, or readiness.
