## 3. Visual semantics bound as doctrine

Carried from the runway and binding on every D1 record and every later W6 artefact:

- **a rendered word is a claim;**
- **colour is a sentence;**
- **a checkmark is a verdict;**
- **grouping, sort order, dashboard position, icon choice, and interaction affordance are all possible claims;**
- **visual language must never mint authority that the governed source does not hold.**

Concretely for the records to operationalise: no green/pass/checkmark/verdict visual may attach to any execution, evidence, or applicability state; ordering may not imply ranking of truth; prominence may not imply confidence the source lacks; an affordance may not invite an act the governance does not permit (a "convert" button on a pending stub would be a claim about ceremony that is false); and the absence of an honest state from a display is itself a barred move — omission is the quietest overclaim.

## 4. Acceptance criteria for W6-D1

W6-D1 is complete when, and only when: all six decision records are accepted and published through full ceremony, each before anything it gates; every one of the runway's fourteen doctrine questions is answered in exactly one record (mapped in §2, verified at each landing); every one of the runway's ten risk bindings is discharged into at least one record's decisions (§5); the visual-semantics doctrine of §3 appears in binding form in DR-W6-01 and DR-W6-04; the deterministic proof obligations of §6 are stated in the records that own them; and no catalogue, grading record, rendered label, or surface exists anywhere in the repository. **A complete D1 is doctrine complete, not capability begun** — W6-D2 still requires its own brief.

## 5. Risk bindings carried into D1

The runway's ten risks, each assigned: **labels minting authority** → DR-W6-01/04. **Visual overclaim** (colour, icon, layout, grouping, checkmarks) → DR-W6-01's prohibited-visual law + DR-W6-04. **Unknowns visually resolved, hidden, softened, or shown as errors** → DR-W6-02 (Q4 law + the omission-is-overclaim rule of §3). **Stub conversion by convenience** → DR-W6-03 (surfacing without conversion; no conversion affordance). **Generative-era drift** → restated here as a D1-wide non-goal: no D1 record may name model output as a rendering input; review surfaces render governed records only. **Catalogue scope creep** → DR-W6-05 (identity law; governed strings only; ceremony for additions). **Surface write-back** → DR-W6-06 (read-only contract with structural proof obligations). **Historical register mismatch** → assigned forward to W6-D3 by the runway; D1's only obligation is that DR-W6-01's vocabulary law leave room for register annotations without permitting silent edits to sealed records. **Dependency temptation** → D1 adds none, and any future surface dependency is its own named fence-crossing record, never a D1 side effect. **Board staleness** → this brief's own landing updates the board in the same commit.

## 6. Proof obligations for the D1 doctrine records

Stated now so no record can land without them: every mechanically decidable law carries a deterministic proof obligation in the record that decides it — the closed display vocabulary as a machine-checkable set; the prohibited-label list as a scannable ban; the prohibited-visual classes stated in checkable terms wherever a rendering artefact later exists; the catalogue-ID namespace grammar as a validator obligation for the class W6-D2 wakes; the review-surface contract as structural proof obligations (no write path, no cache, no state, read-only sources) in the exact style the W5 runtime proofs established. Review-only judgements (register, tone, dignity of `not_evidenced`) are named review-only, never smuggled into mechanical claims — the DR-W4-06 decidability discipline carried to the surface era.

## 7. Non-goals

W6-D1 does not: create the catalogue or any catalogue artefact · allocate any final catalogue ID (illustrative placeholders only, explicitly marked non-final) · grade any review-of-record string or execute D5-T24 · render any label · build any UI, CLI, or review surface · permit surface write-back · change the runtime, any fixture, any evaluation record, the pending ledger, any Lane C status, any Tier 3 row, or any applicability record · contact any model or perform generative evaluation · add any dependency · move E10/vendor, Z4, E12/Z5, or the hosted class · open W6-D2, W6-D3, W6-D4, W6-D5, or W6-D6 · or open W7. The nine pending stubs remain visible and unconverted; the twenty-six `unknown-not-absent` records are displayed by nothing, because nothing displays yet; and no sentence in this brief or its records claims safety, correctness, a pass, conformance, certification, diagnosis, therapy, production readiness, or behavioural proof — in words or in pixels.
