**The runway put a second question after the boundary: of everything the proposed release would reuse, what actually exists, what is only designed, and what is a limitation the release would inherit — and for everything the release proposes to do, what authority it already has and what it still needs. This brief assigns that question to a governed record. It says what the record must read, what it must classify and map, what counts as evidence for each classification, and what the record may not infer. It does not audit. It classifies no component, maps no capability, selects no shell, scope, workflow or criterion, answers no open decision, authorises no baseline record, application, directory, dependency or model contact, and changes the standing of nothing carried from W8 or decided by ADR-0062.**

It does not exist at this landing. ## 2. The question, as the runway assigns it

5. The question is `W9-AR` §5 question 2, carried here unchanged: ***"For each component the release would reuse: is it implemented, tested, published design only, or a carried limitation — re-derived from source?"*** The runway answers its questions *"from published sources and the live repository, never from a planning summary and never from phase closure"* (`W9-AR` §5).

6. The runway's row for W9-D2 (`W9-AR` §6) fixes the shape of the answer: ***"A governed record re-derived from source: every reusable component classified as implemented, tested, published design or carried limitation; every proposed release capability mapped to its declared edge, its governing record, and the exact brief or decision it still needs."*** The row ends: *"No capability is inferred from phase closure"*. The record therefore does two things — **(a)** an inventory of reusable components, each accounted for from source: classified where its evidence establishes a class, and reported as not established where it does not (item 16); and **(b)** a map of proposed release capabilities, each traced to its authority and its outstanding need — and it does them from source.

7. The question comes before scope because a release can be scoped against capabilities that do not exist: the runway names as a tripwire *"any proposed capability described as existing when the source shows design only"* (`W9-AR` §13), and binds every W9 landing to *"no capability claim without a source citation"* (`W9-AR` §14).

## 4. Required audit assignments

The baseline record must do all of the following. **Each is an assignment, not yet a finding, and none is performed here.**

### 4.1 The object, and the commit it describes

11. The record audits the repository at **one named published commit** — the public baseline in force when its candidate is derived, re-grounded live — and every classification it makes is a statement about that commit and no other. It reads four things only: the tracked bytes at that commit; the accepted records that govern them, at the bytes their registry entries bind; the published landing commits, for what their messages record (item 8); and the standing proofs, as they run at that commit. **The three named stale derivatives — the root `README.md`, `docs/governance/registry.md` and the concept overview — are cited, if at all, as what they are, and never as evidence of what exists** (`W9-AR` §12).

12. **The record creates no evidence of behaviour, and its own source-integrity work is not such evidence.** The two are kept apart:
    - **Barred.** The record writes no test and no proof module, authors no fixture, example or demonstration, makes no capture, produces no evidence artefact, and runs no experiment, instrument or exercise that would yield new evidence that a behaviour or a capability exists or works (ADR-0062 Parts E and F). The standing suite and the public-safety scan are run as they stand at the commit, unaltered, and nothing is added to either.
    - **Required, and not barred.** Reading without changing: enumerating the tracked files at the commit; recomputing content hashes against the bytes the registry binds; checking that each cited path, test identity, registry identity and quoted passage is where the record says it is; and establishing the completeness item 14 requires. So is the verification the candidate's own landing owes under `W9-AR` §14: the placeholder probe, the exact binding checks and the ceremony (item 40).

    A working helper used for that reading **reads and counts, and changes nothing it reads**. Its result is a statement about sources and bindings, reported at the reach it actually has and no further; it is never offered as evidence that a behaviour or a capability exists, and it is not release evidence. It is a project artefact and its use is a project run (ADR-0062 decision 3), bound by every rule of construction, exclusion and handling that binds them (ADR-0062 Parts C, D and E). It is no part of any landing census and is not landed. **Where the evidence a classification would need does not exist at the commit, the record reports the gap (item 16). It does not fill it.**

### 4.2 The component inventory

13. **A reusable component** is defined by what it is: a unit of tracked code, of governed data or of accepted specification, present at the audited commit, that a proposed release capability (item 19) would reuse rather than create. The inventory is built from the source and not from the proposed release, and covers:
    - **tracked code** — every tracked file under `engine/`, `runtime/` and `scripts/` is placed in exactly one component, or is stated not to be a reusable component, with the reason;
    - **governed data and registers** that a release could read — the governed string catalogue and the synthetic fixtures among them — each stated as a component or as not one, with the reason;
    - **accepted specifications without code** — every accepted record that specifies behaviour a proposed capability would need, the four room contracts among them;
    - **published limitations** — the engine's limitations and open decisions that `W9-AR` §12 lists, each re-derived from its own source, and any further limitation the sources publish.

    The standing proofs under `tests/` are read as the evidence item 15 requires; a proof is itself an inventory item only where a proposed capability would reuse it.

14. **Completeness is stated, never assumed.** For each source class of item 13, the record says how it established that its inventory is complete, and names any part it could not close. **The completeness account holds every inventory item, classified or not**: an item whose class could not be established stays in it, marked as item 16 requires, and none is left out to make the account whole.

### 4.3 The four classes, and the evidence each needs

15. **The four classes are the runway's, and an inventory item takes at most one of them**: the one its evidence at the audited commit establishes. An **item** is the smallest thing classified — one stated behaviour of tracked code, one stated property of a governed data artefact or register, one specified behaviour of an accepted record, or one limitation. Each class is defined for each kind of thing it can be said of:
    - **implemented** —
      - *of code:* tracked code at the commit performs the stated behaviour, cited by path, with the accepted record that authorised that code; and no standing proof exercising the stated behaviour is cited, because none exists or none reaches it;
      - *of governed data or a register:* the artefact is **materialised** — tracked bytes at a cited path, under the accepted record that governs it and, where the registry binds it, at the bytes its entry binds — and no standing check of the stated property is cited. Materialised says that the artefact is present, in that form, under that record. **It says nothing of any code that would read, render or apply it**: that is a behaviour of code, and an item of its own;
    - **tested** —
      - *of code:* implemented, and a named standing proof exercises the stated behaviour at the commit: the proof module and test identities, what the proof exercises, over what material, against what (a temporary store, an in-process double, a structural parse), on what platform evidence (ADR-0008), and what it does not reach;
      - *of governed data or a register:* materialised, and a named standing check holds a **stated, bounded property** of the artefact at the commit: the check's identity, the property, the bytes it reads, and what it does not reach. A check that an artefact is present, parses or matches its bound hash establishes that property and no other. **Presence alone is never tested**, and a check of a property is never a proof of any behaviour that would use the artefact;
    - **published design** — *of an accepted specification:* an accepted record specifies the behaviour, cited by identity and section, and no tracked code or materialised artefact realises it at the commit; the record states the specification's own standing, including what implementation, if any, it authorises. **Acceptance of a specification is never implementation of it**: a specified behaviour that tracked code or a materialised artefact does realise is classified as that code or that artefact, on its own evidence, with the specification cited as its governing record;
    - **carried limitation** — a limitation, residual, open decision or fixed property that an accepted record or the engine's own account publishes, which a release would inherit and would have to state honestly. A property the sources fix by decision is classified as that property, never as a defect awaiting repair: *"a lost passphrase is a lost vault by decision, with no recovery path and none to be added"* (`W9-AR` §12).

    **Classes are stated item by item.** A component whose behaviours or properties stand at different classes is divided into items, each classified on its own, and **no component is given a class as a whole because one of its items has it**. A component's limitations are separate inventory items, each linked to the component it limits. **A tested classification states the behaviour a proof exercises or the property a check holds, and nothing more**: it is never evidence that the component is fit for, adequate to or ready for a release, and never evidence that any function of an application exists.

16. **The class the evidence establishes, and no more.** The record gives each item the class its cited evidence establishes, states that evidence, and names what the evidence does not reach. **The four classes are not a scale**: design is not a lesser implementation, a limitation is not a failed one, and none is a higher or a lower grade of another. The rule is against overclaiming, and it licenses no underclaiming: **a behaviour or a property whose tested status the evidence establishes within a stated bound is reported as tested within that bound**, whatever the class of the rest of its component. **Nothing is classified beyond its evidence** by inference, analogy, naming, a module's docstring, a record's description of intended or future work, or the existence of a related component. A component an accepted record says will exist is published design until code exists; code that no standing proof exercises is implemented, not tested.

    **A gap in evidence is a finding, not a stop.** Where a proof, a check or a citation that a class would need does not exist at the commit, the record keeps the item, reports what the existing sources establish, names the gap, states the route by which it could be closed where the sources identify one and says so where they do not (item 21), and goes on. **The absence of a proof is never permission to write one** (item 12).

    **Where no class can be established** — the sources support none of the definitions of item 15, or tracked code is present and the accepted record that authorised it cannot be found — the item takes no class and is not dropped. It stays in the inventory and in the completeness account of item 14, marked *not established from the sources*, with what the tracked bytes and the records do show, what is missing, and the route by which the question could be decided (items 18 and 21). ***Not established* is the absence of a classification: it is not a fifth class, it is no finding about the item's maturity, and no class is assigned to close it.**

17. **Authority, recorded and never granted.** For each implemented or tested item the record names the accepted authority under which its code was built or its artefact materialised, and the standing rules that bind any reuse of it: the engine import cap (ADR-0009), the runtime's separation from the sealed engine in both directions (ADR-0024 decision 4), and the directory and dependency fences (`W2-D3` rules 4 and 5). **It grants no authority, and where it cannot find one it says so under item 16 and supplies none.** Reuse of any component in an application needs that application's own runway, brief and Tier F authorisation (`W9-AR` §1), and the record says so wherever it matters.

18. **Discrepancies are reported, not repaired.** Where an accepted record's account of a component and the tracked bytes disagree, the record reports both sources, classifies by what the tracked bytes and the standing proofs establish (item 16), and states the route for deciding the difference under the two-tier rule (`W2-D3` rule 2). It corrects no record and changes no code.

### 4.4 The capability map

19. **The capabilities.** Every proposed release capability the runway names, each listed once and none merged away or added from preference: the north star and its usefulness requirement with no model and no device (`W9-AR` §3); the steps of the common governed journey and the four room tasks (`W9-AR` §10); the draft criteria RC-01 to RC-14 (`W9-AR` §15); and the rights a person holds over their own records (`W9-AR` §7). For each row the record shows the source text it traces to. A capability the runway states as one requirement stays one row, whether it would use several edges, one or none (item 20).

20. **For each capability, the record states:**
    - **(a) whether it proposes a governed flow, and every declared edge that flow would use** — with the source text and the reason, one of three positions:
      - **a governed flow with declared edges:** every edge of W1-D1 §5 the capability would use, each by its identity — **all of them, and never one standing for several**;
      - **a governed flow, or a part of one, with no declared edge:** stated as **no declared edge**, naming the part of the flow that has none (item 22);
      - **no governed flow established:** either the requirement, as the runway states it, is a property of the build, of its documentation or of its upkeep, and not a movement, reading, processing or disclosure of governed data — stated as **edge not applicable**, with the reason; or the sources do not let the record say whether the capability proposes a governed flow — stated as *not established from the sources*, with what is missing.

      The first two are not exclusive: a composite capability carries, on its one row, its declared edges for the part that has them and *no declared edge* for the part that has none. **Not applicable is said of a requirement and never of data.** Whatever handling of governed data meeting that requirement would in fact involve is a flow: it takes the first or the second position, on the same row, and nothing exempts it;
    - **(b) the grant position**, for each flow under (a): the grant type of W1-D2 §2 under which it would proceed, where the sources require a grant; or that the sources require a grant and W1-D2 §2 provides no type for it, **a gap, stated as one and filled by nothing**; or that the act is a right and takes no grant; or, where the sources do not establish whether a grant is required, *not established from the sources*. Where (a) is *edge not applicable*, so is (b), for the same reason: **a requirement that proposes no flow needs neither an edge nor a grant by being a requirement.** No grant is entered as needed, available or sufficient for a flow that has no declared edge;
    - **(c) the governing record** or records;
    - **(d) the components it would reuse**, each item with its class from section 4.3, or marked as not established;
    - **(e) ADR-0062's finding** for that element, where its Part J assesses one, carried exactly as published and with the threshold of its decision 36, and never re-assessed;
    - **(f) the exact brief, decision or route it still needs** (item 21).

21. **What item 20(f) may name, and what item 16 may name as a route, is a closed set:**
    - a W9 deliverable by its runway row — W9-D3, W9-D4, W9-D5 or W9-D6 — named and not opened;
    - a decision record the corpus already names (item 28);
    - **a decision record or other governed route that the sources expressly require and have not yet named or allocated** — entered by the rule that requires it, with its source, and with no identity invented or reserved for it. A flow with no declared edge is entered here (item 22);
    - a later runway, brief and Tier F authorisation, for anything that would be implementation, a directory or a dependency;
    - the human reviewer's own judgement, where a source reserves one;
    - **no remaining need identified in the published sources** — entered only where the record has looked for one and the sources show none;
    - **a need whose route the published sources do not identify** — entered as that, with what is missing.

    **The last two are different findings and are never merged**: the first says the sources show nothing further owed; the second says something is owed and the sources do not say by what route. **A need is never left out because the record that would meet it has no identity yet.** **The record identifies needs; it decides, authorises, ranks, schedules and sequences none of them**, except where the runway or ADR-0062 already sets an order, which it carries.

22. **An undeclared flow does not exist, and a requirement that proposes no flow acquires no edge.** *"A flow that does not appear in §5 does not exist."* (W1-D1 §0), and *"Consent authorises edges; it cannot create them."* (W1-D2 §0). Where a capability proposes a governed flow, in whole or in part, for which W1-D1 §5 declares no edge, the record says that the flow does not exist and is prohibited, and that a new edge would need a decision record with a constitutional check before any consent question could be asked (W1-D1 §0; W1-D2 §0) — **never that a grant or a person's consent could supply one**. That prohibition is about flows, and it is kept exactly as strict as its sources make it. **It is not turned into a rule that every capability must have an edge**: a requirement of installation, of accessibility or of maintenance is not, by being a requirement, a governed flow, and the record invents no edge and no grant for it. **Rights are not grants:** a person's access to, export of and erasure of their own records are never mapped to a grant (`W9-AR` §7).

23. **The map is not the workflow trace.** The record identifies each capability's applicable edges, governing records and outstanding need at the level of the capability. **Naming the edges a composite capability would use is not tracing it**: which declared edge, grant type, record class and room-contract clause permits each input, each processing step, each persistence and each output is W9-D4's (`W9-AR` §6), and the record performs none of it.

### 4.5 What the record may not infer

24. **No capability and no readiness from closure or from counts.** *"Closure is not capability"* (`W9-AR` §2), and *"Closure authorises nothing beyond closure."* (`W8-CR` §11). No phase closure, completion record, board status, count of proofs, green suite or silent scan is evidence that any component or capability exists, works or is ready: *"existing suite results remain evidence about their own subject: they establish nothing about an application that does not exist."* (`W9-AR` §15).

25. **No application capability from evidence that proves only an engine or a runtime.** The engine's own account is *"No UI, no CLI, no network, no model contact — one API, its tests, and nothing else."* (`engine/README.md`), and of its own text it says *"this document summarises and cites, it decides nothing."* The runtime's transmission layer records *"No recipient exists in this milestone."* (`runtime/transmission.py`). A proof that the engine or the runtime behaves as its record requires is evidence about that engine or runtime, over its own test material, and nothing more. **A capability that would need a caller, an interface, a lawful store of human acts, a review path, a persistence decision or an installable build is classified by its parts, and is never described as existing because its parts are.**

26. **No fitness verdict.** Whether any component is fit for a release is a question for the release contract and the release decision, never for this record. The record makes no adequacy, readiness, safety, correctness, `certif-` or `complian-` statement about any component or capability.

### 4.6 Decision 14, preserved open

27. **The record relies on neither answer to ADR-0062 decision 14.** No row is assessed as lawful, feasible, needed or excluded on an assumption about it. **Every capability that presupposes a reference build that a person could install and operate carries decision 14 as an open dependency**, stating both consequences decision 15 sets out and choosing neither, and every landing that would hold or publish a build still owes its own judgement of effect in a fresh P4b act (ADR-0062 decision 15). No classification, count or map row is offered as bearing on decision 14 in either direction.

    **No dependency is resolved, ranked, converted or reclassified by being carried.**

### 4.8 Decidability and proof obligations

29. **Every rule of the record declares whether a machine can decide it**, under the discipline that *"an obligation is not called mechanical until a working check has been demonstrated against real bytes."* (ADR-0047 decision 24). The existence at the audited commit of every cited path, test identity and registry identity, and the coverage of item 13's tracked code, are of a kind a machine could check. Where the candidate checks them, it does so by the source-integrity work of item 12, and reports that work as what it is: a working helper's result at its actual reach, and not a standing proof. **Whether each classification is right, whether a proof's reach is stated truly, whether the inventory and the map are complete, and whether each dependency is carried at its true standing are review-only**, and no green result is evidence for them in any degree.

30. An obligation over artefacts that do not yet exist is specified and assigned to the landing that first creates such an artefact, and is *"a debt, not a receipt"* (ADR-0046 decision 28). **Review-only duties are named review-only and are never reported as mechanical results.**

### 4.9 Inheritance

31. State what the record hands W9-D3 to W9-D7 as fixed input — the inventory and its classes at the audited commit, with every gap and every item not established reported as such, the capability map and the dependency register — and what it does not touch. **A later deliverable that finds a classification wrong reports it with its route; none upgrades a classification by drafting, by scope or by a criterion.** A classification is true of the commit it names: a later deliverable that relies on one at a later commit re-grounds it first.

## 5. How the record may not reach a finding

32. The baseline record may not: classify from a planning summary, from phase closure, from a count of proofs, from a derivative or from the wording of this brief (`W9-AR` §5) · describe a capability or component as existing where the source shows design only, or state what exists without a source citation (`W9-AR` §13 and §14) · infer an application capability from an engine or runtime proof (item 25) · rely on either answer to ADR-0062 decision 14, or re-decide anything ADR-0062 decides · create a test, a proof module, a fixture, an example, a demonstration, a capture or an evidence artefact, or run any experiment that would yield new evidence of a behaviour or a capability — the source-integrity work and the landing verification of item 12 are neither · assign a class to close a gap, or leave out an item it cannot classify (item 16) · invent an edge or a grant for a requirement that proposes no flow, or treat *edge not applicable* as exempting any handling of governed data (items 20 and 22) · amend, correct or reinterpret any accepted record, or change any code — a needed change is reported with its route under the two-tier rule (`W2-D3` rule 2) · decide scope, the supported environment, the application shell, any workflow, the integration posture or any release criterion, which are W9-D3's to W9-D6's · decide the E9 export format, vault layout, profile export, whole-vault export or recovery codes (`W9-AR` §11 and §12) · create any edge, grant type, record class, authority state or sensitivity class, or present consent as able to supply an edge · name, describe, elaborate or depend upon the external side the fixed invariant refers to, or design, describe or prepare any real-person use · turn the W8-D5 finding into a readiness finding or the W8-D6 decision into a permission · state whether model contact occurred · or make any diagnostic, therapeutic, crisis, legal, `certif-`, `complian-`, production-readiness, safety, correctness or approval claim.

33. **A component that is convenient to call ready is not thereby ready, and a gap that is inconvenient is not thereby absent.** The record is judged on whether each classification and each map row follows from the evidence it cites, and on nothing else.
