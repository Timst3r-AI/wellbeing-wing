## 2. Objective

The doctrine must make the three-way distinction deterministically enough to govern later evaluation design: a case author must be able to construct true-delta, negative-control and genuinely-inconclusive cases from the doctrine without guessing its meaning, and a human reviewer must be able to reach any of the three dispositions lawfully, including against expectation. The doctrine must make these distinctions **without collapsing governance into textual difference, intuition, tone, confidence, stylistic similarity or grader preference**.

## 3. Required doctrine assignments

The doctrine record must settle all of the following. Each is an assignment, not yet law.

### 3.1 Positive governance-delta law

Define what makes a difference a governance-relevant delta. **The governing test must be whether a governed property changes in a way that changes downstream obligations, permissions, authority, treatment or state — never merely whether two texts differ.** The governed-property law must be capable of covering, at minimum: authority · provenance · permission · persistence · memory treatment · decision status · boundary crossing · attribution · review state · and other properties lawfully added to the doctrine.

**The property list may be growable, never casually extensible, and the sequencing is binding:** a new governed property may be recognised only through a governed doctrine amendment or other express authority that **lands before any evaluation case relies on that property**. A case, calibration run or finding may **reveal** that the law needs extension; it may not itself extend the law.

### 3.2 Positive non-delta law

Define `no_governance_delta` **affirmatively**: the doctrine must state positively when language can change while governed meaning remains unchanged — never merely "not a delta". Surface features that may vary include tone, warmth, confidence, verbosity, phrasing, formatting, politeness and explanatory depth. The doctrine must preserve both sides of the crucial distinction:

- **Surface features are not governed properties in themselves.** A difference in one of them proves nothing either way — the instrument may not manufacture governance significance because language looks different, and may not presume governance equivalence because only style appears to have moved.
- **If a surface change alters an actual governed property under the downstream-obligation test, the governed-property change controls.** Surface difference ≠ governance difference — **and surface classification ≠ immunity from governance consequence.**

### 3.3 Mandatory `review_inconclusive` law

Define `review_inconclusive` as a **valid completed disposition** — not hesitation, not failure, not a pending state, and not a weaker form of either other result. It must be **required** wherever the admissible evidence does not justify `governance_delta_present` or `no_governance_delta`, under **positive conditions rather than discretionary uncertainty language**. At minimum the doctrine must handle: evidence genuinely insufficient to determine whether a governed property changed · reviewer-visible source evidence that admits more than one materially different governed interpretation · **unresolved conflict within admissible source evidence** · and cases where deciding either direction would require importing intent, context or authority not lawfully present in reviewer-visible evidence. Neither substantive disposition may be reached by elimination.

### 3.4 Closed disposition vocabulary

The doctrine must close the W8 case-level vocabulary to exactly **`governance_delta_present` · `no_governance_delta` · `review_inconclusive`**. No pass/fail vocabulary, no winner, no preference, no confidence-derived disposition, no model grade, no approval state. **No aggregate score, success rate, model verdict or overall result may override, replace or pressure a case-level disposition.** The doctrine must also fix the vocabulary's prospective, non-retroactive relation to the published ADR-0053 law (section 3.10).

### 3.5 Evidence-admissibility doctrine

The doctrine must distinguish evidence classes clearly enough that later case authors and reviewers cannot silently promote context into evidence, preserving the central rule: **reviewer disposition is grounded only in admissible source evidence.** Other material may exist for provenance, construction, calibration or post-review analysis, but may not silently become grounds for the disposition. The doctrine must specify: **(1)** what counts as reviewer-visible source evidence; **(2)** what supporting structural and provenance information may be visible; **(3)** what authoring information exists but is withheld from disposition; **(4)** what post-disposition calibration material may subsequently be revealed.

### 3.6 Blinding law

The doctrine must require deliberate reviewer blinding. **Before disposition, the reviewer-visible source must exclude the authoring class and authoring intent.** Source grounding is limited to the case variants themselves and whatever declared reviewer-visible fields the doctrine expressly permits. The reviewer must not be able to learn, from metadata or otherwise, whether a case was authored as a true governance delta, a negative control, or a genuinely inconclusive case. Authoring intent may be revealed **only after disposition**, for calibration and instrument evaluation under the later calibration law. **The authoring label must never become evidence for the disposition it was designed to test.**

### 3.7 Anti-collapse law

The doctrine must bind, at minimum: **confidence ≠ authority · verbosity ≠ governance significance · warmth ≠ permission · explanation ≠ evidence · textual difference ≠ governance difference · textual similarity ≠ governance equivalence · absence of explicit wording ≠ absence of governed effect · mechanical agreement ≠ human acceptance · uncertainty ≠ failure.** The builder may improve wording, organisation or derivation; **none of these distinctions may be weakened or omitted.**

### 3.8 Human-review law

The doctrine must preserve human judgment as a governed act rather than disguise it as mechanical proof, settling: the exact human review question · reviewer-visible evidence · the permissible disposition vocabulary · the meaning of each disposition · that a completed `review_inconclusive` is a valid human judgment · that mechanical results may support integrity but may never be presented as the semantic human judgment · that no implementer may infer, pre-fill, default or bulk-select a human disposition · and that later evaluation reporting must preserve individual case decisions. The standing rule binds: **mechanical evidence ≠ human authority.**

### 3.9 Case independence

Each case is dispositioned on its own lawful evidence. A reviewer may not decide a case because another case was classified a certain way, because a desired class distribution is expected, because the evaluator needs a negative control, because an aggregate would otherwise look odd, or because earlier cases suggest what the current author's intention probably was. **Case ordering must not become hidden evidence.** Any aggregate analysis happens only after the complete set of individual dispositions required for that session exists.

### 3.10 Relationship to W7

The doctrine is **prospective, for the W8 instrument**, built on the accepted W7 human-review architecture without silently rewriting its history. It may explain why W8 needs stronger discrimination than the W7 instrument. It must not: alter any W7 GER · revise any W7 disposition · reclassify the twenty-six historical cases · claim the W7 human review was invalid · turn the W7 reviewer observation into retroactive doctrine · or resolve any W7 carried seam merely because W8 matures the instrument. **W7 remains historically true.**

## 4. The four bound corrections

The following four corrections, fixed at the consolidated design review, are requirements on the doctrine record and must be visibly incorporated in it:

1. **Prior-authority requirement for governed-property growth** — a governed amendment or other express authority lands before any case relies on a newly recognised property; cases, calibrations and findings reveal, never extend (section 3.1).
2. **Surface features are not themselves governed properties** — and a surface change that alters a governed property is controlled by the property change under the downstream-obligation test (section 3.2).
3. **Evidence conflict means unresolved conflict within admissible source evidence** — source evidence is the grounding class, and the inconclusive conditions are never framed as conflict between evidence classes (section 3.3).
4. **Pre-disposition blinding excludes authoring class and intent**, with intent revealed only post-disposition for calibration (sections 3.5–3.6).
